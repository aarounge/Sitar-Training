Month 1: Foundation of Melodic Music
Week 1: The Seven Swaras
Complete Swar System

The 7 Shuddha Swaras

Sa (Shadaj), Re (Rishabh), Ga (Gandhar), Ma (Madhyam), Pa (Pancham), Dha (Dhaivat), Ni (Nishad)

Sa and Pa are achal (immutable). Re, Ga, Dha, Ni can be komal. Only Ma can be tivra.

Tabla Connection

Swaras are like tabla bols - basic vocabulary. Just as you learned Ta, Dhin, Dha, Ge systematically, learn Sa, Re, Ga, Ma systematically. Both are building blocks of musical expression.

Three Saptaks (Octaves)

  • Mandra Saptak: Lower octave (Sa. Re. Ga. - dots below)
  • Madhya Saptak: Middle octave (Sa Re Ga - no marks)
  • Taar Saptak: Upper octave (Sa' Re' Ga' - dots/marks above)

12 Semitones (Practical Shruti)

Sa | Re♭ | Re | Ga♭ | Ga | Ma | Ma# | Pa | Dha♭ | Dha | Ni♭ | Ni | Sa'

One octave = 12 semitone steps. Each raag selects 5-7 notes from these 12.

Week 2: Komal & Tivra Notes
Altered Notes Complete Study

Four Komal (Flat) Notes

  • Re♭: Serious, devotional, morning character (Bhairav, Todi)
  • Ga♭: Emotional, folk-like (Kafi, Bageshri, Asavari)
  • Dha♭: Somber, grave (Bhairav, Darbari, Asavari)
  • Ni♭: Folk-influenced, accessible (Khamaj, Kafi)

One Tivra (Sharp) Note

  • Ma#: Bright, uplifting, evening character (Yaman, Marwa, Poorvi)
  • Ma# is THE defining characteristic of evening raags!
Compare: Sa Re Ga Ma Pa (all natural)
With: Sa Re♭ Ga♭ Ma Pa (komal Re & Ga)
With: Sa Re Ga Ma# Pa (tivra Ma)
Week 3: Introduction to Thaat
Thaat System Foundation

What is Thaat?

Parent scale framework of 7 notes used to classify raags. Created by Pandit V.N. Bhatkhande. 10 thaats cover all major Hindustani raags.

The 10 Bhatkhande Thaats

  1. Bilawal: Sa Re Ga Ma Pa Dha Ni (all natural)
  2. Kalyan: Sa Re Ga Ma# Pa Dha Ni (sharp Ma)
  3. Khamaj: Sa Re Ga Ma Pa Dha Ni♭ (flat Ni)
  4. Bhairav: Sa Re♭ Ga Ma Pa Dha♭ Ni
  5. Poorvi: Sa Re♭ Ga Ma# Pa Dha♭ Ni
  6. Marwa: Sa Re♭ Ga Ma# Dha Ni
  7. Kafi: Sa Re Ga♭ Ma Pa Dha Ni♭
  8. Asavari: Sa Re Ga♭ Ma Pa Dha♭ Ni♭
  9. Bhairavi: Sa Re♭ Ga♭ Ma Pa Dha♭ Ni♭
  10. Todi: Sa Re♭ Ga♭ Ma# Pa Dha♭ Ni

Month 2: Raag Fundamentals
Week 1: What is a Raag?
10 Components of Raag
  1. Thaat: Parent scale classification
  2. Jati: Number of notes (audav=5, shadav=6, sampoorna=7)
  3. Aroh: Ascending scale pattern
  4. Avaroh: Descending scale pattern
  5. Vadi: Most important note (king note)
  6. Samvadi: Second most important (queen note)
  7. Pakad: Characteristic identifying phrase
  8. Samay: Time of performance
  9. Rasa: Emotional content
  10. Chalan: Melodic movement patterns

Tabla Connection

A raag is like a complete tabla composition system - not random notes, but structured with rules. Like a kayda has theme, variations, and specific development patterns, a raag has pakad, aroha-avaroha, and movement rules.

Week 2-4: Raag Yaman Complete Study
Yaman - Your First Raag

Raag Yaman Profile

Thaat: Kalyan

Notes: Sa Re Ga Ma# Pa Dha Ni Sa'

Jati: Sampoorna-Sampoorna (all 7 notes)

Vadi: Ga, Samvadi: Ni

Samay: Evening (6-10 PM)

Rasa: Shringar (romantic, devotional)

Aroh: Ni Re Ga Ma# Dha Ni Sa'
Avaroh: Sa' Ni Dha Pa Ma# Ga Re Sa
Pakad: Ni Re Ga Ma#, Ga Re Sa

Why Learn Yaman First?

  • Most popular evening raag
  • Clear, unambiguous character
  • Uses all 7 notes (complete learning)
  • Foundation for many other raags
  • You've accompanied it countless times on tabla!

Yaman Practice Routine

  • Week 1: Aroha-avaroha 30x daily with tanpura
  • Week 2: Pakad phrase 50x daily, emphasize Ga
  • Week 3: Simple Yaman alankars in teen taal
  • Week 4: Learn one traditional Yaman bandish

Week 3-4: Time Theory (Samay Chakra)
24-Hour Raag Cycle

The Time Theory System

Raags are classified by time of day/night. Based on mood created by note combinations.

Morning Raags (6AM-10AM)

  • Characteristics: Re♭ and Dha♭ (komal)
  • Thaats: Bhairav, Todi
  • Examples: Bhairav, Todi, Lalit
  • Mood: Serious, devotional, introspective

Late Morning (10AM-12PM)

  • Examples: Bilawal, Alhaiya Bilawal
  • Mood: Bright, balanced

Afternoon (12PM-3PM)

  • Examples: Multani, Sarang
  • Mood: Hot, intense (rarest time)

Evening (6PM-10PM)

  • Characteristics: Ma# (tivra Madhyam)
  • Thaats: Kalyan, Poorvi, Marwa
  • Examples: Yaman, Puriya, Marwa, Bhoopali
  • Mood: Bright, uplifting, romantic

Night (10PM-12AM)

  • Examples: Bageshri, Yaman Kalyan
  • Mood: Deep, romantic

Late Night (12AM-3AM)

  • Examples: Malkauns, Darbari Kanada
  • Mood: Very serious, meditative

Why Time Matters

Ma# = Evening: Sharp Ma creates brightness, suits sunset mood

Re♭, Dha♭ = Morning: Flat notes create seriousness, suits dawn mood

All natural = Midday: Complete brightness suits noon

Month 4: Jati & Raag Classification
Week 1-2: Understanding Jati System
Complete Jati Classification

What is Jati?

Number of notes used in aroha (ascending) and avaroha (descending)

Three types: Audav (5), Shadav (6), Sampoorna (7)

The 9 Possible Jati Combinations

  1. Audav-Audav: 5 notes ascending, 5 descending
  2. Audav-Shadav: 5 ascending, 6 descending
  3. Audav-Sampoorna: 5 ascending, 7 descending
  4. Shadav-Audav: 6 ascending, 5 descending
  5. Shadav-Shadav: 6 ascending, 6 descending
  6. Shadav-Sampoorna: 6 ascending, 7 descending
  7. Sampoorna-Audav: 7 ascending, 5 descending
  8. Sampoorna-Shadav: 7 ascending, 6 descending
  9. Sampoorna-Sampoorna: 7 ascending, 7 descending

Examples of Each Type

Audav-Audav (Pentatonic):
Raag Bhupali: Sa Re Ga Pa Dha Sa' (omits Ma, Ni)
Raag Malkauns: Sa Ga♭ Ma Dha♭ Ni♭ Sa' (omits Re, Pa)
Shadav-Shadav (Hexatonic):
Raag Deshkar: Omits one note in both directions
Sampoorna-Sampoorna (Heptatonic):
Raag Yaman: All 7 notes both ways
Raag Bhairavi: All 7 notes both ways

Why Omit Notes?

  • Creates distinctive character - less is often more!
  • Avoids conflict with other raags
  • Makes raag easier to identify
  • Creates specific emotional quality

Tabla Connection

Like how some tabla compositions use only specific bols (avoiding certain strokes to create character), audav/shadav raags omit notes deliberately. The constraint creates identity!

Week 3-4: Vakra (Zigzag) Movements
Understanding Vakra Sanchar

What is Vakra?

Vakra = crooked/zigzag. Instead of straight Sa-Re-Ga, the movement goes Sa-Ga-Re or Re-Sa-Re

Creates unique melodic character and helps distinguish similar raags

Types of Vakra Movements

Straight (Samvadi): Sa Re Ga Ma Pa
Vakra Example 1: Sa Ga Re Ma Pa (skips Re, returns)
Vakra Example 2: Sa Re Sa Re Ga (oscillates before proceeding)

Famous Vakra Raags

  • Raag Yaman: Often starts Ni Re Ga (vakra - skips Sa)
  • Raag Malkauns: Very vakra in movements
  • Raag Shree: Highly vakra, complex movements

Important: Vakra movements must be learned specifically for each raag - they're part of the raag's identity!
