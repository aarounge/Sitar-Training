🎵 Bilawal Alankars Mastery

A 4-week structured plan for tabla players learning sitar. Bilawal is the foundational thaat (scale) in Hindustani music - equivalent to the Western major scale. This plan builds your finger technique, rhythm coordination, and melodic foundation.

Understanding Bilawal Thaat

Scale Structure: Bilawal uses all natural (shuddha) notes with no flats or sharps.

Sa Re Ga Ma Pa Dha Ni Sa'

In Western terms: C D E F G A B C

Why Bilawal First? It's the most straightforward scale with no altered notes, making it perfect for building clean technique and muscle memory. Think of it as the "sa sa dhin dhin" of sitar - fundamental and essential.

🥁 Tabla Connection: Just as you practiced countless hours of basic bols (Ta, Dha, Dhin) before complex compositions, these alankars are your foundational "bols" on sitar. Your discipline with tabla palta transfers directly here!

Week 1: Foundation & Single-String Mastery
Goal: Clean individual notes, proper mizrab technique, steady rhythm
▼
Day 1-2: Single Note Clarity
Session 1: Open String Practice 15 min
  • Sit in proper posture, sitar on left foot, relaxed shoulders
  • Practice striking Ma string (main playing string) with mizrab
  • Focus: Even volume, consistent angle, clean tone
  • Strike once per second - count "1, 2, 3, 4" mentally
Session 2: Fret Pressing 20 min
  • Find Sa (first note) on main string - press just before fret
  • Press Re, Ga, Ma - one at a time, hold for 5 seconds each
  • Check: No buzzing, clean sound, finger perpendicular to string
  • Repeat 10 times for each note
Tip: Press with fingertip just behind fret (not on top). Use minimal pressure - just enough to get clean sound. Relax between presses.
Day 3-4: Sa Re Ga Ma (Ascending)
Alankar 1: Simple Ascent
Sa Re Ga Ma | Pa Dha Ni Sa' ||
Play each note twice at 40 BPM
Morning Practice 25 min
  • Set metronome/tabla app to 40 BPM, vilambit (slow) teen taal
  • Play Sa (strike with mizrab), hold briefly, then Re
  • Continue: Ga, Ma, Pa, Dha, Ni, Sa' (higher octave)
  • Pause. Breathe. Repeat 5 times
  • Focus on even gaps between notes
🥁 Tabla Connection: This is like practicing "Ta Ti Ta Ti" at slow speed. You already know how to maintain steady tempo - apply that skill here!
Evening Review 15 min
  • Record yourself playing the alankar
  • Listen back: Check rhythm consistency and note clarity
  • Identify any unclear notes and practice those transitions
Day 5-7: Sa' Ni Dha Pa (Descending)
Alankar 2: Simple Descent
Sa' Ni Dha Pa | Ma Ga Re Sa ||
Full Practice Routine 30 min
  • Warm up: Ascending alankar 3 times (from Day 3-4)
  • New: Descending from Sa' back to Sa
  • Play slowly, ensure left hand relaxes between notes
  • Combine: Ascend then descend without stopping
  • Repeat complete cycle 5 times
Common Mistake: Rushing! Your tabla training helps you avoid this. Stay locked to the beat. Quality over speed.
Week 1 Success Criteria: By end of week, you should play Sa-Re-Ga-Ma-Pa-Dha-Ni-Sa'-Ni-Dha-Pa-Ma-Ga-Re-Sa smoothly at 40 BPM with clean notes and no rhythm breaks.
Week 2: Pattern Variations & Speed Building
Goal: Learn 3 classic alankar patterns, increase to 50 BPM
▼
Day 1-2: Two-Note Patterns
Alankar 3: Pairs
Sa Re | Re Ga | Ga Ma | Ma Pa ||
Pa Dha | Dha Ni | Ni Sa' | Sa' Sa' ||
Sa' Ni | Ni Dha | Dha Pa | Pa Ma ||
Ma Ga | Ga Re | Re Sa | Sa Sa ||
Pattern Learning 30 min
  • Start at 40 BPM - one note per beat
  • Ascending pairs: Sa-Re, Re-Ga, Ga-Ma, etc.
  • Notice the overlap: each note appears twice
  • Practice ascending 10 times, then descending 10 times
  • Then combine without pause
🥁 Tabla Connection: Similar to "Dha Dhin | Dhin Dha" patterns - overlapping elements. You're familiar with this concept!
Day 3-4: Three-Note Patterns
Alankar 4: Triplets
Sa Re Ga | Re Ga Ma | Ga Ma Pa | Ma Pa Dha ||
Pa Dha Ni | Dha Ni Sa' | Sa' Sa' Sa' ||
Descending reverse pattern...
Triplet Mastery 35 min
  • Three-note groups moving up the scale
  • Keep tempo steady - use metronome!
  • Each group flows into the next smoothly
  • Build finger independence and memory
Day 5-7: Speed Increase & Consolidation
Progressive Speed Work 40 min
  • Day 5: Increase metronome to 45 BPM
  • Practice all 4 alankars learned so far at new speed
  • Day 6: Try 48 BPM if comfortable
  • Day 7: Reach 50 BPM goal
  • If struggling, stay at lower tempo - accuracy first!
Speed Building Tip: Increase by only 2-3 BPM at a time. Just like tabla riyaz, patience builds technique. Speed comes from relaxation, not tension.
Alankar 5: Four-Note Pattern (Bonus)
Sa Re Ga Ma | Re Ga Ma Pa | Ga Ma Pa Dha ||
Learn if Week 2 feels comfortable
Week 2 Success Criteria: Comfortable with 4-5 different alankar patterns at 50 BPM. Smooth transitions between notes. Beginning to play without looking at fingers constantly.
Week 3: Rhythmic Integration & Teen Taal
Goal: Play alankars in proper teen taal structure, coordinate with tabla
▼
Day 1-3: Teen Taal Structure
Taal Coordination 35 min
  • Review teen taal: 16 beats (4+4+4+4)
  • Play simple alankar with sama (beat 1) emphasis
  • Sa should land on sama, complete phrase by next sama
  • Use tabla app or recording in teen taal at 50 BPM
  • Clap the taal while playing mentally first
🥁 Tabla Gold! This is where your tabla degree shines! You intimately know teen taal structure. Now you're playing the melodic counterpart. Feel the sam, respect the structure.
Teen Taal Alankar (16 beats)
X - - - | 2 - - - | 0 - - - | 3 - - - ||
Sa Re Ga Ma | Pa Dha Ni Sa' | Sa' Ni Dha Pa | Ma Ga Re Sa ||
(1 note per beat = 16 beats total)
Day 4-5: Doubling Speed (Dugun)
Dugun Practice 30 min
  • Keep tabla at 50 BPM, but play 2 notes per beat
  • This is dugun - double speed layakari
  • Start with simple Sa-Re-Ga-Ma pattern
  • Maintain clarity even at faster note rate
Layakari Tip: You know dugun from tabla! The concept is identical. Subdivide beats clearly in your mind.
Dugun Example (2 notes per beat)
Sa-Re Ga-Ma Pa-Dha Ni-Sa' | Sa'-Ni Dha-Pa Ma-Ga Re-Sa ||
(Now 32 notes in 16 beats)
Day 6-7: Ektaal & Jhaptaal (Bonus)
Exploring Other Taals 25 min
  • Try same alankars in ektaal (12 beats)
  • Adapt patterns to fit taal structure
  • Or jhaptaal (10 beats) - more challenging!
  • This develops taal flexibility
🥁 Advanced Integration: You can play tabla in multiple taals. Same flexibility applies to sitar! Experiment with fitting alankars into different taal frameworks.
Week 3 Success Criteria: Comfortable playing alankars in proper teen taal structure with sama awareness. Can play basic dugun. Feel rhythmic confidence growing.
Week 4: Refinement, Expression & Performance
Goal: Add musicality, play from memory, record final performance
▼
Day 1-2: Dynamics & Expression
Adding Musicality 30 min
  • Play alankars with volume variation (soft to loud)
  • Emphasize sama with slightly stronger strike
  • Experiment with slight pauses before returning to Sa
  • Make it musical, not just mechanical exercise
Expression Tip: Think of how tabla has bhari and khali (filled and empty) sections. Create similar light and shade in your sitar alankars.
Day 3-4: Memory & Flow
Playing Without Notation 35 min
  • Practice all learned alankars without looking at notes
  • Close your eyes and feel the movements
  • String together 3-4 different alankars in sequence
  • Create your own variations based on patterns learned
Personal Composition Challenge
Create your own alankar pattern using Bilawal notes
Make it fit perfectly in teen taal (16 beats)
Example: Sa Ga Re Ma | Ga Pa Ma Dha | Pa Ni Dha Sa' | etc.
Day 5-6: Speed & Stamina
Final Speed Push 40 min
  • Push tempo to 60 BPM if comfortable
  • Practice for longer durations - build endurance
  • 10-minute continuous alankar sessions
  • Notice any tension - stay relaxed
Avoid Injury: Stop if fingers hurt. Sitar requires different muscles than tabla. Build gradually. Warm up and cool down.
Day 7: Performance & Recording
Final Showcase 45 min
  • Record yourself playing 5 different alankars
  • Use tabla accompaniment (app or recording)
  • Play as if performing - with confidence and musicality
  • Listen back and celebrate your progress!
  • Note areas for continued improvement
🥁 Full Circle: Imagine accompanying yourself! You're now experiencing both sides of the melodic-rhythmic dialogue. This deepens your understanding of both instruments.
Week 4 Success Criteria: Can play 5+ alankar patterns from memory at 60 BPM with proper taal structure. Notes are clean, rhythm is steady, musicality is developing. Ready to move to next challenge!

Essential Resources & Tools

  • iTanpura or Tanpura Droid: Electronic tanpura for drone accompaniment - tune to your sitar
  • Metronome Plus or Soundbrenner: Precise metronome apps with tabla sounds
  • iTabla Pro or Tabla Guru: Tabla accompaniment in different taals and speeds
  • Voice Recorder: Daily recording essential for tracking progress
  • Sargam Note Finder app: Helps identify notes on sitar
  • YouTube Channels: Search "Sitar Alankars" for visual demonstrations

After Week 4: What's Next?

Immediate Next Steps:

  • Apply same alankar patterns to other thaats (Kafi, Bhairav)
  • Increase speed to 80-100 BPM gradually over next month
  • Begin learning Raag Yaman using your alankar foundation
  • Start simple meend (string pulling) exercises between notes
  • Practice jhala technique on Sa-Pa alankars
🥁 Your Unique Advantage: As a tabla player, you have perfect rhythmic sense. This allows you to progress faster than typical beginners. You can coordinate complex patterns more quickly. Use this advantage - but remember melody requires different patience than rhythm!
