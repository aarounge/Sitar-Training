Six complete compositions in classical sargam notation — ready to perform, each designed around your tabla foundation.

Notation Guide

Sa Tonic — always your anchor
Pa Fifth — second anchor note
Ma# Tivra Madhyam (sharp Ma)
Re♭ Komal note (flat)
Sa' Upper octave note
Ni. Lower octave note
X Sam / Beat markers
— Hold / rest this beat

Composition 01

Raag Yaman
Yaman Pakad Gat
The most recognisable Yaman melody — that signature Ma# phrase audiences hear and immediately feel. This is your go-to opener.
Teen Taal · 16 beats Beginner 90 sec
Sthayi — Main Theme
X Ni Re Ga Ma# | 2 Ga Re Sa — | 0 Ni — Dha — | 3 Pa — — —
↑ This opening phrase IS Yaman. Audiences recognise it in the first two bars.
X Pa Ma# Ga Re | 2 Ga Ma# Dha Pa | 0 Dha — Pa — | 3 Ma# Ga Re Sa
Antara — Upper Octave
X Dha Ni Re' Ga' | 2 Ma#' Ga' Re' Ni | 0 Dha — Pa — | 3 Ma# Ga Re —
X Re' Ga' Ma#' Ni | 2 Dha Pa Ma# Ga | 0 Re Sa — — | 3 Sa — — —
↑ Return to Sa on the final beat 13 — lands beautifully before repeating Sthayi.
Tihai Ending (Repeat 3×, lands on Sam)
Ga Ma# Pa Dha | Ga Ma# Pa Dha | Ga Ma# Pa — | X Sa — — —
↑ Classic tihai! You know this structure from tabla — three equal phrases landing on sam.
🥁 Tabla Player's Edge

You already know this tihai concept perfectly — three equal repeating phrases that land on sam. You've played hundreds of tihais on tabla. Now it's melodic. The maths is identical. When you perform this, your tihai will be precisely placed in a way most sitar beginners can never achieve.

Opening Alap

Before the gat, play free-tempo: Ni Re Ga Ma# … hold Ma# for 4-5 seconds. The audience feels Yaman before you even start the rhythm section.

Emphasise Ga

Ga is the vadi (king note) of Yaman. Whenever it appears, play it slightly louder. This is what makes Yaman sound like Yaman.

Sam Arrival

Make every sam (X beat) arrival feel intentional. A slight accent on beat 1 of each cycle signals musical authority to any musicians and listeners present.

Tempo

45 BPM vilambit. Slow is sophisticated. Audiences find slow Yaman more moving than fast. Resist the urge to speed up.

Composition 02

Raag Bhupali
Pahadi Dhun
Five notes only. Pure pentatonic beauty. Bhupali is the most universally loved raag — audiences who've never heard classical music are immediately moved by it.
Teen Taal · 16 beats Beginner 75 sec
Scale — Only 5 Notes (No Ma, No Ni)
Sa Re Ga Pa Dha Sa' | Sa' Dha Pa Ga Re Sa
↑ 5 notes total. Every single note is safe. No Ma#, no Ni. Impossible to play a wrong note!
Sthayi
X Sa Ga Re Sa | 2 Pa — Dha Pa | 0 Ga — Re — | 3 Sa — — —
X Re Ga Pa Dha | 2 Pa Ga Re Sa | 0 Ga — Re — | 3 Sa — — —
Antara
X Dha Sa' Re' Ga' | 2 Re' Sa' Dha Pa | 0 Dha Ga Re — | 3 Sa — — —
↑ The antara leaps to Sa' — that single jump creates huge emotional impact with minimal technique.
Tihai Ending
Sa Re Ga Pa | Sa Re Ga Pa | Sa Re Ga — | X Sa — — —
🥁 Tabla Player's Edge

Bhupali is pentatonic — the same structure as many folk and devotional scales you've accompanied on tabla. Your muscle memory for the rhythmic flow in these simpler scales will make this piece feel almost effortless. Use the simplicity to focus entirely on expression rather than notes.

Secret Weapon

Bhupali sounds like it could be a film song or folk melody. Non-classical audiences connect to it emotionally far faster than Yaman. Use it for mixed audiences.

Pauses are Music

The rests (—) are as important as the notes. Bhupali breathes. Don't fill every beat. Let the notes ring and decay — that resonance is the raga.

Evening Raag

Play this at your Saturday open mic right as evening begins. Timing matches the raag's samay. A small detail that musicians in the audience will appreciate.

For Short Sets

If you only have 90 seconds, Bhupali is your piece. It opens and resolves quickly without feeling rushed.

Composition 03

Raag Bilawal
Prabhat Geet
All natural notes — bright, balanced and technically forgiving. Bilawal is the cleanest possible showcase of your sitar tone. Perfect for daytime or morning performances.
Rupak Taal · 7 beats Beginner 75 sec
Rupak Taal Structure (3+2+2)
0 Sa Re Ga | 2 Ma Pa | 3 Dha Ni || … 7 beats total
↑ Rupak starts on khali (0) — unusual! Creates an elegant, floating feeling different from teen taal.
Sthayi
0 Sa Re Ga | 2 Ma Pa | 3 Pa Ma || 0 Ga Re Sa | 2 — Sa | 3 Sa —
0 Re Ga Ma | 2 Dha Pa | 3 Ma Ga || 0 Re Sa Ni | 2 Sa — | 3 — —
Antara
0 Pa Dha Ni | 2 Sa' Ni | 3 Dha Pa || 0 Ma Ga Re | 2 Sa — | 3 — —
🥁 Tabla Player's Edge

Rupak taal starts on khali — every tabla player knows this taal. You'll feel the 3+2+2 structure instinctively. This is where non-tabla sitar players often drift; you will be rock-solid. That rhythmic precision in Rupak is immediately noticeable and impressive to listeners.

Rupak Announcement

Before playing, you can say: "This is in Rupak Taal — seven beats." It's a small detail that immediately signals classical knowledge to any musically literate audience member.

Natural Notes = Pure Tone

Bilawal is your tone test. No altered notes to hide behind. Play it clean, and your sitar tone itself becomes the art.

Composition 04

Raag Khamaj
Khamaj Thumri Riff
Khamaj is the most crowd-pleasing raag — it underlies countless film songs and semi-classical pieces. That single flat Ni gives it a warmth and folk familiarity that non-classical audiences adore.
Keherwa · 8 beats Beginner 60 sec
Khamaj Scale (Flat Ni only)
Sa Re Ga Ma Pa Dha Ni♭ Sa' | One komal note only — very easy!
Main Theme (Keherwa 8 beats: X — — — | 2 — — —)
X Sa Ga Pa Ga | 2 Re Sa Ni♭ Sa || X Re Ga Pa Dha | 2 Pa Ga Re Sa
X Pa Dha Ni♭ Sa' | 2 Ni♭ Dha Pa — || X Dha Pa Ga Re | 2 Sa — — —
↑ Flat Ni♭ creates the characteristic Khamaj warmth — that note alone connects to dozens of beloved songs.
Repeat & Fade (for open mic ending)
X Sa Ga Pa — | 2 Sa — — — || …hold final Sa and let it ring …
🥁 Tabla Player's Edge

Keherwa — you know it in your sleep. Every qawwali, bhajan, and semi-classical piece you've accompanied used Keherwa. Playing Khamaj over Keherwa will feel like coming home. Your natural theka sense will lock this melody into the groove perfectly.

Most Accessible Raag

If you don't know your audience's musical background, play Khamaj. Its folk-adjacent warmth creates connection with everyone from classical scholars to people hearing sitar for the first time.

Keherwa Energy

Keherwa taal has natural forward momentum. Use it to create a lively, celebratory feeling rather than the meditative quality of teen taal compositions.

Composition 05

Raag Yaman
Yaman Vilambit — The Slow Burn
Ultra-slow, meditative Yaman. Each note held and allowed to bloom. This is for moments when you want the audience to sit in silence and just listen. The most mature-sounding performance option you have right now.
Ektaal · 12 beats Intermediate 3 min
Ektaal (12 beats: X 0 2 0 3 4 | 0 0 5 0 6 0)
Alap Phrases (Free Tempo — Before Ektaal Begins)
Sa — — — | Ni Re Ga — | Ga — Ma# — | Ga Re Sa —
↑ Hold each phrase. This is 30 seconds of music with 4 notes. That takes courage — and sounds extraordinary.
Sa — — — | Pa — — — | Ma# Ga Re — | Sa — — —
Ektaal Sthayi (2 notes per beat — vilambit gat)
X Ni-Re 0 Ga-Ma# 2 Ga-Re 0 Sa-— 3 Ni-— 4 Dha-Pa 0 —-— 0 Ma#-Ga 5 Re-Sa 0 —-— 6 Sa-— 0 —-—
↑ 12 beats, 2 notes each = 24 note slots. Many are rests. The space IS the music.
Development — Exploring Upper Register
X Pa-Dha 0 Ni-Re' 2 Ga'-— 0 Ma#'-Ga' 3 Re'-Ni 4 Dha-Pa 0 Ma#-Ga 0 Re-Sa 5 —-— 0 —-— 6 Sa-— 0 —-—
🥁 Tabla Player's Edge

Ektaal — 12 beats. You know this deeply. The unusual beat structure (X 0 2 0 3 4 | 0 0 5 0 6 0) with its multiple khalis sounds complex to audiences but is completely natural to you. Playing Yaman in Ektaal instead of the typical Teen Taal immediately signals advanced classical knowledge. Most beginners never attempt it.

Silence is Skill

The hardest part of vilambit is resisting the urge to fill every beat. A long held note followed by a perfect three-note phrase is worth more than constant motion. Trust the rests.

For Serious Contexts

Use this version when performing for musicians, at classical events, or whenever you want to signal deep classical understanding. It says "I know what I'm doing" without a single word.

Alap First, Always

Never jump straight into the gat for this piece. The free-tempo alap is essential — it establishes the mood and prepares the listener. Minimum 45 seconds of alap before the taal begins.

Composition 06

Raag Bageshri
Raat ki Raagini
Late night raag. Flat Ga and flat Ni create a deep, melancholic beauty that is unlike anything else in the repertoire. Perfect for the final piece of an evening set. Audiences go very quiet for Bageshri.
Teen Taal · 16 beats Intermediate 2 min
Bageshri Scale (Flat Ga and Ni — omits Re in aroh)
Aroh: Sa Ga♭ Ma Dha Ni♭ Sa' | Avaroh: Sa' Ni♭ Dha Ma Ga♭ Re Sa
↑ Re appears only descending! This asymmetry is what makes Bageshri sound so distinctive.
Sthayi
X Ma Dha Ni♭ Dha | 2 Ma Ga♭ Re Sa | 0 Ga♭ — Ma — | 3 Dha — — —
X Ni♭ Dha Ma Ga♭ | 2 Re Sa Ga♭ Ma | 0 Dha — Ni♭ — | 3 Sa — — —
↑ The Ma-Dha-Ni♭ combination is the soul of Bageshri. That cluster is immediately recognisable.
Antara — Reaching for Sa'
X Dha Ni♭ Sa' Ni♭ | 2 Dha Ma Ga♭ Ma | 0 Dha — Ni♭ Sa' | 3 — — — —
X Ni♭ Dha Ma Ga♭ | 2 Re Sa — — | 0 Ga♭ Ma Dha — | 3 Sa — — —
🥁 Tabla Player's Edge

Bageshri is a night raag — it's best played at your Saturday open mic late in the evening set, or as a closing piece. The emotional depth of Bageshri combined with your rhythmic precision creates a memorable performance. You've accompanied this raag before; you know its weight. Now carry that weight melodically.

The Closing Piece

Always end your set with Bageshri if you play it. It's emotionally conclusive. Audiences leave with that melancholic resonance in their ears — they'll remember you.

Re Only Descending

The strict rule: Re appears only in the avaroha (descent). Never use Re going up. This restriction is what makes Bageshri distinctive — honour it and the raag will reward you.

Ma-Dha-Ni Cluster

The three-note phrase Ma-Dha-Ni♭ is your pakad. Return to it often. It's the musical signature that makes the audience feel "this is Bageshri" — even if they don't know the name.

Introduce It

"This last piece is a late-night raag called Bageshri — traditionally performed after 9pm." That sentence alone creates anticipation and shows depth of knowledge.
